\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Decision points in compound systems.}
LLM applications are increasingly built as systems of models, retrievers and tools rather than as one model call~\cite{zaharia2024compound}, and each junction in such a system is a decision.
Prior work optimizes each junction on its own terms; we compare one class of component, a standalone decider, across three junctions on one harness and ask when it earns its call.

\textbf{Semantic caching.}
GPTCache~\cite{bang2023gptcache} serves a stored answer when the embedding similarity of two prompts passes a threshold.
Later systems improve the matcher: MeanCache~\cite{gill2025meancache} trains the similarity model with federated learning and tracks conversational context, LangCache-style embedders are fine-tuned on domain paraphrase pairs~\cite{gill2025advancing}, ensembles combine several embedding models~\cite{ghaffari2025ensemble}, and SCALM~\cite{li2024scalm} clusters real chat logs to decide what to cache.
vCache~\cite{schroeder2025vcache} learns a threshold per cached prompt with a user-set error bound.
All of these still decide a hit by similarity, which recent work shows is not the same as validity~\cite{zhang2026similarity}.
Closest to our guard, Krites~\cite{singh2026asynchronous} sends near-miss neighbours to an LLM judge, off the serving path to hide its latency.
We evaluate a judge that reads both queries inline and find that a non-generative decision model is cheap and fast enough for that role, while a frontier LLM can cost more than the answers it saves.

\textbf{Model routing and cascades.}
FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} cascades models with a learned scorer, AutoMix~\cite{aggarwal2023automix} lets a small model verify itself, and Hybrid LLM~\cite{ding2024hybrid}, RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm} and RouterDC~\cite{chen2024routerdc} train routers on quality or preference labels.
Shnitzer et al.~\cite{shnitzer2023large} frame routing as per-model correctness prediction from benchmark records, the recipe our trained baseline follows, and Dekoninck et al.~\cite{dekoninck2025unified} unify routing and cascading and identify the quality estimator as the deciding factor.
Cascade deferral by confidence is well understood for classifiers~\cite{jitkrittum2023when} and has been extended to token-level uncertainty in LMs~\cite{gupta2024language}.
Benchmarks and evaluations, from RouterBench~\cite{hu2024routerbench} to RouterEval~\cite{huang2025routereval}, RouterArena~\cite{lu2025routerarena} and LLMRouterBench~\cite{li2026llmrouterbench}, repeatedly find a large gap to the oracle and routers that struggle to beat simple baselines: a tuned kNN matches complex learned routers~\cite{li2025rethinking}, many routers level off at a common plateau~\cite{lu2026routing}, and the small model's own uncertainty often beats a separate router~\cite{ramirez2024optimising}; see~\cite{varangotreille2026doing} for a survey.
We add general, untrained deciders to this comparison: none replaces a classifier trained on the small model's record, and even that classifier reaches an APGR of only 0.649, against 0.5 for a random mix.

\textbf{Tool use and split agents.}
Tool-using LLMs decide when and which tool to call inside their own generation~\cite{schick2023toolformer,patil2024gorilla,qin2024toolllm}, and are evaluated on BFCL~\cite{patil2025bfcl} and the $\tau$-bench family~\cite{yao2024taubench,barres2025tau2}.
A line of work splits the agent into roles: a planner and workers that avoid re-reading observations~\cite{xu2023rewoo}, a planner, caller and summarizer built from small models~\cite{shen2024small}, a planner that emits a DAG of parallel calls~\cite{kim2024llmcompiler}, a separately trained planner for long-horizon tasks~\cite{erdogan2025planandact}, and small models for tool selection with a larger model for execution~\cite{erdogan2024tinyagent,chen2024octopus}, a direction recently argued for in general~\cite{belcak2025small}.
These splits pay through parallelism, shorter contexts or weak executors.
Our in-loop arms test the narrowest split, a per-turn tool decision in front of a frontier generator, and find it never beats the single call; with a weak generator it appears to help, consistent with~\cite{shen2024small}.
Following~\cite{kapoor2025agents}, we report agent results as cost against reward.

\textbf{Getting a discrete decision from an LLM.}
Constrained decoding makes generated choices well formed~\cite{willard2023efficient,zheng2024sglang,dong2025xgrammar}, but the model still prefills the full context and decodes, and format restrictions can cost accuracy~\cite{tam2024speak}.
Fine-tuned small classifiers still beat zero-shot generative models on text classification~\cite{bucher2024finetuned}, and a classification head on an LLM backbone can replace generation for labels~\cite{li2023label}, the design \kev\ follows with a pointer head over a variable option set.
\jev\ and \kev\ package this as a general decision model; to our knowledge this is the first independent measurement of either across decision points with cost, latency and energy.

\textbf{Calibration of stated confidence.}
LMs are fairly well calibrated in their token probabilities on multiple-choice questions~\cite{kadavath2022language}, and verbalized confidence can be trained~\cite{lin2022teaching} or elicited~\cite{tian2023just}, but off-the-shelf verbalized confidence is typically overconfident~\cite{xiong2024can} and good uncertainty tends to need training~\cite{kapoor2024large}.
We see the same: the small LLMs' stated confidences are the worst calibrated in agent control (ECE 0.286 and 0.599), while the decision model's probabilities are the best (0.029).

\textbf{Energy of inference.}
Per-query energy has been measured for generative LLM inference~\cite{samsi2023words}, across task-specific and general-purpose models~\cite{luccioni2024power}, in benchmarks built on NVML energy counters~\cite{you2023zeus,chung2025mlenergy}, and at production scale with full-stack accounting~\cite{elsworth2025measuring}; serving systems lower it by reconfiguring clusters~\cite{stojkovic2024dynamollm}.
Sampled power readings can miss much of a short run~\cite{yang2023parttime}, so we integrate the cumulative counter and report gross and idle-subtracted energy per decision.

\textbf{Label errors and LLM relabeling.}
Label errors are common in test sets and can change model rankings~\cite{northcutt2021pervasive}, including in LLM benchmarks~\cite{gema2025done,vendrow2025platinum}.
LLMs can match or beat crowd workers as annotators~\cite{gilardi2023chatgpt}, but agreement with humans varies by task~\cite{zheng2023judging,bavaresco2025llms}, which is why we treat our LLM-corrected labels for the two vCache datasets as a limitation.
