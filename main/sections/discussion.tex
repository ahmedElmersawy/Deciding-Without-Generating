\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Two conditions.}
Across the three decision points, a standalone decider paid for its call only when both of the following held.

\emph{The decision avoids a generation.}
In agent control a generator runs after every decision, so the cheapest design lets that generator decide.
At the cache guard and the router, one outcome of the decision skips the expensive call.

\emph{The evidence is in the input.}
Whether two queries ask the same thing can be read off the two queries.
Whether a particular small model will answer a prompt correctly cannot be read off the prompt.

The cache guard meets both conditions and is where the decision model wins.
Agent control fails the first and routing the second.
We expect the conditions to transfer to other decision points: input guardrails and intent classification meet both, while ``should the agent retry?'' sits in front of a generation and likely does not.

\textbf{The decision must be much cheaper than what it saves.}
A frontier LLM was the most accurate guard on GSM-Plus and still raised total cost above running without a cache.
The relevant quantity is the price of a decision relative to the price of the generation it may avoid, weighted by how often it avoids one.

\textbf{What the vendor claims amount to.}
The price claim holds: \jev\ cost 22 to 48 times less per decision than the frontier model in our runs, and its latency was a steady 0.18\,s.
The quality claim holds at the cache guard and partly for single agent decisions (7 points below the frontier model).
The implied claim that it can replace an LLM at any decision does not hold: in the agent loop and at the router, a cheaper or better alternative existed.
Its probabilities were the best calibrated of any decider we measured, which is what makes cascades around it work.

\textbf{Open decision models.}
\kev\ shows that the paradigm is reproducible but not uniformly: it matched \jev\ on judging whether two queries are the same request and collapsed on multi-step airline policy.
On equal hardware its energy advantage over a same-size generative LLM making a constrained choice was about 20\%, not an order of magnitude.
A constrained LLM call emits very few tokens, so most of the work in both cases is reading the input.

\textbf{Limitations.}
\jev\ is proprietary and versioned; our numbers describe version 1.13 through one gateway in one period.
Agent control uses one domain, with labels and a generator from one frontier model.
The corrected labels of the two vCache datasets come from an LLM and were not checked by hand.
Hosted deciders have cost and latency measurements but no energy.
RouterBench's models are from 2023; the router predicts those models' recorded correctness, so the structure of the result should hold, but the size of the gap may differ for current models.
We did not fine-tune \kev\ or any LLM for a decision point; a tuned decision model might close the routing gap, at which point it is a trained classifier with a different architecture.
